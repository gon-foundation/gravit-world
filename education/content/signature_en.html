What is a digital signature

How can we know that a message was written by this very person, and that not a single letter was changed on the way? That is what a digital signature is for. This lesson shows it at work, right in the browser.

Learning goals

  • Pupils explain the difference between a private key and a public key.
  • Pupils see that changing one letter breaks the signature.
  • Pupils understand why anyone can check a signature but only the owner can make it.

Three ideas

Private key

Like a personal seal. Only the owner has it and never shows it. It makes the signature.

Public key

Like a sample of the seal that everyone knows. Anyone can use it to check a signature, but nobody can sign with it.

Fingerprint (hash)

A short number computed from the text. Change one letter, and the fingerprint becomes completely different.

Questions for the class

  1. What happened to the fingerprint when you changed one letter?
  2. If someone intercepts the message and changes "Friday" to "Monday", will the receiver notice?
  3. Why must the private key never be shown, while the public key can be published?

Where we meet it

Signatures protect phone updates, bank transfers, electronic documents and records of decisions that others check. Anyone with the public key can check a signature. That is its strength: nobody has to take anyone's word for it.

Lesson plan for teachers

  1. 5 min. Ask the class: in the old days, how did people prove a letter came from the king? (A seal.)
  2. 10 min. The three ideas. Draw on the board: the private key signs, the public key checks.
  3. 15 min. Experiment in pairs: one signs, the other changes a letter, both check.
  4. 10 min. The three questions.
  5. 5 min. Summary: "A signature does not say the text is true. It says who signed it and that it was not changed."
