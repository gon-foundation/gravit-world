How to take back your data

Photos, videos, emails, game scores: all of it sits on other people's computers, "in the cloud". This lesson teaches pupils to know where their things are and how to take them back.

Learning goals

  • Pupils understand that "the cloud" is someone else's computer.
  • Pupils know they have a right to a copy of their data.
  • Pupils can download their files and check that the copy is complete.

Three rules

1. The cloud is someone else's computer

Convenient, but the owner writes the rules. Space can run out; terms can change.

2. You have a right to a copy

In Europe, the UK and Switzerland the law gives everyone the right to know what data is held about them and to receive a copy (GDPR and UK GDPR, Articles 15 and 20; Swiss Data Protection Act, Articles 25 and 28).

3. A copy counts only once it is checked

Downloaded? Open the files and compare number and size. A broken download looks finished, but it is not.

A story to discuss

Someone had used the same email account for many years. One day emails stopped arriving: "storage full". It turned out the phone had been uploading videos to cloud storage on its own, and email and storage shared one limit. The screen offered one thing: buy more space. To free space without paying, they had to:

  1. find what was using the space (hidden in the settings);
  2. download the largest videos to their own computer;
  3. notice that a big archive had stopped halfway, and download the files in smaller parts;
  4. check the copy: number of files, size, the videos play;
  5. only then delete the files in the cloud.

Questions for the class: which step mattered most? What would have happened if the files had been deleted before the copy was checked?

Exercise: check the copy

A practice example. There were 3 files in the cloud. What was downloaded? Click a line.

✔ video_1.mp4: 405 MB in the cloud, 405 MB on the computer

✘ video_2.mp4: 321 MB in the cloud, 96 MB on the computer

✘ video_3.mp4: 183 MB in the cloud, no file on the computer

The hint appears here.

The 3-2-1 rule

Keep important things in 3 copies, on 2 different kinds of storage, with 1 copy in another place (for example at a grandparent's home or in another cloud).

Lesson plan for teachers

  1. 5 min. Ask the class: where are your photos? What if the service shuts down?
  2. 10 min. The three rules. Write them on the board.
  3. 10 min. The story and the two questions.
  4. 10 min. The exercise "check the copy".
  5. 10 min. Each pupil writes a 3-2-1 plan for one important thing.

Homework

With an adult, find the "download my data" or "export" function in one service (email, photos, a game). Write down how many steps it took and how easy it was to find. Do not delete anything.
