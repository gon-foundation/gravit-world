Purpose, values, integrity

A manifesto for verifiability. Version 1.1, 11 November 2026: adds the section on machine intelligence; version 1 of 9 November 2026 stays in the change history.

Three foundations of a new era

1. Human dignity is inviolable

"Die Würde des Menschen ist unantastbar" (German Basic Law, Article 1(1)). People created artificial intelligence. Progress means a better life for people, in Africa, in Chile, everywhere. Without people and without the next generation, technology has no future.

2. Verification

Every claim that affects a person's life can be checked.

3. Publication

Methods, rules and results are published and open to everyone.

Roots: since 2015

We did not start yesterday. In 2015 the Decipial Foundation in Geneva set out six decentralized principles. They are still published at decipial.org:

  1. The digital future of people is in danger: centralized systems are failing, and personal data needs protection built on decentralized systems.
  2. Open decentralized systems democratize the economy and give individuals greater opportunities.
  3. Decentralized organizations have become a reality: value moves away from the centralized intermediary.
  4. Insurance, welfare, longevity and quality of life are linked to the platform of decentralization.
  5. More and more people take part in the global economy through reliable decentralized funds.
  6. Efficient governance, accountability and transparency through a democratic, decentralized platform.

In 2015 we asked who owns the data. Today we ask how everyone can check for themselves. Eleven years of work by one team show that this is not a fashion and not a chase for money.

Purpose

To create value for future generations through verifiability, provability and openness. We look ahead 10, 20 and 30 years. We do not claim to see further, and we say so.

Values

  1. Verifiability. Not "trust us" but "check it yourself": tool, data, rule, result.
  2. Provability. A claim without proof has the status "stated by its author", and no other.
  3. Openness. Methods, code and results are open, our mistakes included.
  4. People own what is theirs. Their data, their attention, their time. Leaving is as easy as joining.
  5. Verification like air and water. The basic protocol, the rules and the checking tool are open and free for everyone, as the open protocols of the internet once were. Paid services may exist only on top of this base, never instead of it.
  6. An honest price. What has been announced is never changed after the fact. Money is not the purpose; a payment only shows that something is genuinely needed.

Integrity

Reaching the purpose must not step over the values. Keeping the values must not deny integrity.

  • Order of decisions: purpose, then the test against the values, then the integrity of the record. A step that serves the purpose but breaks a value is not taken.
  • History cannot be erased. A mistake is corrected in the open; the earlier version stays on the record.
  • We test ourselves harder than others. We ran one hundred million cases first against our own code and published the gap we found together with its fix.
  • Independence is shown, not declared. Two implementations by one team do not make a check independent. Rebuilds by others do.

The end of an era

For twenty-five years, since the internet boom, the digital world has rested on trust in the interface. Over that time a set of practices became common: bundled quotas, risky defaults, deadline pressure, hidden exits, consent that nobody reads, the person as the product. We name practices, not companies or people, and we do not judge intentions: such a judgement needs proof, and proof is what we ask of others.

These practices are coming to an end, because the means of checking now exist: a signature anyone can verify; a log that cannot be quietly rewritten; open code that can be rebuilt; a rule under which a second checker reaches the same result.

Theses for 10, 20, 30 years

2036

Every important automated decision (on money, access, diagnosis) comes with a record that can be recomputed. Exporting one's own data in one step is normal. Schools teach checking as basic literacy.

2046

People hold their own health passport and see every access to it. A decision affecting a person's freedom can be reproduced from the same evidence. A third party can check an AI's answer.

2056

Trust is measured, not declared. A generation raised with verification sees interface manipulation as a relic of the last century.

Machine intelligence and human responsibility

Gravit does not restrict what machine intelligence may think, invent or say. It requires that any machine action with consequences for people, property, rights or infrastructure be observable, attributable, independently verified and subject to human authority. A more capable machine does not take over the responsibility of the people who build and use it.

A signal is not yet a meaning, a meaning is not yet an intent, and an intent is not yet a permission. Confidentiality is legitimate; control that escapes oversight is not.

This principle is not only stated, it is tested. The open Capability Gate permits nothing that is not listed. A capability at the frontier is used for one exact action only, and only after an independent verifier returns a Verified Fact for that action. A forbidden capability can be reopened only by several distinct authorities together, and even then it must pass verification. Every decision is written to a journal that cannot be quietly rewritten. Each of these rules has a test anyone can run. Status: draft 0.1, not yet externally reviewed.

The role of Gravit

The Gravit Open Network Foundation builds the protocol and tools of verifiable trust: a verifier in two implementations, open test sets, draft standards at the IETF. Gravit Education, Academy and Research teach verification from the first school year. Gravit does not seek fame. We share our experience and bring together those who think about the coming decades.
