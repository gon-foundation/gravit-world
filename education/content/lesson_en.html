How to read a window with buttons

Every day the screen shows us windows: "Update?", "Buy storage?", "Allow?". This lesson teaches pupils to ask a window five questions before they click.

Learning goals

  • Pupils tell a message (what happened) apart from pressure (what they are pushed to do).
  • Pupils find every option in a window, including the hidden ones.
  • Pupils can rewrite a window so that it is honest.

Five questions for any window

1. What actually happened?

Find the one sentence with a fact. Set everything else aside.

2. What does it want from me?

Buy, allow, agree, stay? Name it in one word.

3. Which button is big and which is small?

The big, coloured button usually helps whoever made the window. Find every option, even the grey and tiny ones.

4. Is there a deadline or a threat?

"In 5 days", "last chance". Is the deadline real? What happens if you wait a day and ask an adult?

5. Where is the exit, and where are my things?

Can I close the window, say no, take my files with me? If the exit is hard to find, that is an answer too.

Task: rewrite the window honestly

In pairs. Draw the same window so that all options are the same size, the fact comes first and the deadline is explained. Example:

Storage full (5.2 GB of 5 GB)

New files and emails are not saved. If you do nothing, old files may be deleted in 5 days.

Lesson plan for teachers

  1. 5 min. Ask the class: which window did you see on a screen this week? What did you click?
  2. 10 min. The five questions. Write them on the board.
  3. 10 min. Practice window: the class answers the five questions, then looks at the hints.
  4. 15 min. Pairs rewrite the window honestly. Two pairs present.
  5. 5 min. Summary: "A big button is not the right button. First the fact, then the decision."

Homework

With an adult at home, find one window (phone, computer, TV) and answer the five questions. Do not photograph personal data: name, address, password.
